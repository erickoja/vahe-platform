For jewellers

Jewellery workshop software, built by a jeweller

By Eric Kojadelian, VAHÉ Jewellery

Most software sold to jewellers was written by people who have never sat at a bench. It treats a custom ring like a product on a shelf, when in reality it is a job that moves through casting, setting, polishing and a dozen small steps before it becomes an invoice. Jewellery workshop software should be built around that reality. This is what to look for, and what I built into Workshop Pilot for my own studio.

It should quote the way you actually cost a piece

A real quote is not a single number you pull out of the air. It is metal by weight at today's rate, the centre stone and any accents, your bench time, setting, casting and printing fees, findings and extras. Good workshop software adds all of that up for you, shows the markup and the margin before you send, and turns it into a proposal the client can accept online. If quoting lives in a spreadsheet or your head, something always gets left off, and it is usually the small stuff that eats your profit.

It should track a job through the bench

Once a job is on, you need to see where every piece is at a glance: what is waiting on approval, what is being cast, what is on the bench for setting, what is polished and ready for collection. A board that mirrors your real stages, from enquiry through to collected, means nothing gets forgotten and no client is left wondering. That is the difference between a busy workshop and a chaotic one.

It should handle repairs, not just custom jobs

Repairs are the bread and butter of most workshops, and they are exactly the jobs that slip through the cracks: a ring sizing taken in over the counter, a chain solder, a re-tip. Workshop software should let you take a repair in quickly, quote it, track it and invoice it with the same tools as a full custom build, so the small jobs are as tidy as the big ones.

It should keep your clients and their history in one place

Every quote, job, proposal, payment and repair for a client should sit under that client, so when they walk back in a year later you can see everything you have made for them. That history is how a one-off customer becomes a repeat one. A generic tool scatters it; software built for a workshop keeps it together. (More on that in our guide to a CRM for jewellers.)

It should get you paid

The job is not done until the money is in. Look for deposits handled properly, invoices you can send with your own Stripe, PayPal or bank payment link, live balances so you always know what is owed, and a clean export to hand your bookkeeper or import into MYOB or Xero. The point of the software is not admin for its own sake, it is fewer unpaid jobs and less time chasing.

Above all, it should feel like it was made for the bench

The best sign that workshop software will actually help you is that it speaks your language, carats and millimetres, cast versus fabricated, claw and pavé and bezel, not generic sales-pipeline jargon. Workshop Pilot is shaped by real bench work because a working jeweller built it, and it stays out of your way rather than turning your craft into data entry.
